# Skill: Content Research and Editorial Production

## Who we serve
First-time U.S. homeowners searching for a simple way to plan essential upkeep, understand routine tasks and avoid missing seasonal work. U.S. climate and housing stock vary: don't pretend every state or house needs the same plan.

## Topic selection
For each proposed piece record: homeowner problem, exact search intent, proposed original contribution, source availability, hazards, search competition, useful internal link and update cadence. Prioritize one genuinely good guide or tool over ten thin variations.

## Primary-source research
Prefer:
- energy.gov and ENERGY STAR for relevant efficiency/maintenance guidance;
- EPA for moisture, air, water and environmental guidance;
- CPSC / FEMA / ready.gov for safety and preparedness;
- local government, state extension services, code authorities and manufacturers for region-/model-specific claims.
Use primary documents and link to the precise source page; record access/review dates. Secondary sources can add context, not replace critical verification.

## Draft requirements
- One real reader question answered directly, followed by an appropriate amount of detail.
- Explicit conditions: house type, climate, appliance model, age, physical skill, local rules.
- Original output: planner, checklist, worked calculation, decision tree or clearly better organization of evidence.
- Accurate provenance for numbers; never invent survey data, field tests, testimonials, expert reviewers or author credentials.
- Proper image rights. Original SVGs and diagrams should explain a task, not serve as wallpaper.
- Honest datePublished and dateModified; update only when substantially changed.
- Disclose AI assistance where a reader would reasonably expect clarity; identify the accountable publisher/editor.
- No mass location doorway pages, copying other sites, or generalized advice presented as legal/building-code authority.

## Workflow and stop conditions
Research brief -> source ledger -> first draft -> fact review -> safety review -> usability review -> publish -> schedule refresh. Flag unsupported statements and potentially hazardous procedural claims for human review. Automated production may prepare drafts but never silently assert a safety-critical article is expert-reviewed.
