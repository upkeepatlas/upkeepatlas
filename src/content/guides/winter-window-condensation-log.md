## First identify where the moisture appears

Condensation forms when moisture in air meets a sufficiently cool surface. Winter weather can cool window glass and frames, but moisture on or around a window can also reflect water entry or other conditions. **Do not assume every wet window is harmless condensation.**

| What you can observe safely | Record this distinction | What it does *not* prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Droplets on the room-facing glass | Which pane, edge or corner; time of day | That indoor humidity is the only cause |
| Moisture on the exterior face | Whether it disappears as conditions change | That the window is defective |
| Fog apparently trapped between panes | Whether either accessible surface is dry | That a particular seal or component failed without inspection |
| Wet sill, trim or wall next to glass | Whether it follows precipitation, thaw or morning cold | That the glass is the entry point |
| Persistent staining, swelling or musty odor | Exact location and whether it is spreading | A specific mold type or hidden leak source |

For a broader comparison of condensation, suspected leakage and between-pane fogging, use our [condensation-versus-water-leak guide](/guides/condensation-versus-water-leak/).

## A seven-day winter window log

Choose one or two windows that you can observe from a **dry, stable indoor floor**. Name them by room and direction (for example, “north bedroom, left window”). Take photos from the same angle; avoid identifying details such as street addresses or valuables. Do not lean outside, climb, remove trim, open glazing units or use electrical devices near standing water.

### The three observation moments

1. **Morning:** Note whether droplets are present before the room warms, and whether they sit on the glass, frame, sill or adjacent wall.
2. **After ordinary household activity:** Record whether cooking, showering, laundry or use of a humidifier occurred. Do not deliberately create humidity or run water to test a suspected leak.
3. **Later in the day:** Note whether the visible moisture decreased, persisted or spread. A change is useful context, not proof of cause.

If you already have a room thermometer or hygrometer, record its displayed temperature and relative humidity **with the device location**. Do not treat one reading as a diagnosis. EPA guidance recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60%, ideally 30–50% where feasible, to help manage mold and moisture; cold-window condensation can still occur at lower readings depending on surface temperature. See [EPA's mold and moisture guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## What patterns mean for follow-up

| Repeated observation | Sensible next action |
| --- | --- |
| Brief room-side droplets only on cold mornings, no wet surrounding material | Log the pattern; consider normal moisture-management habits consistent with your home's ventilation and equipment guidance |
| Water regularly collects on wood, drywall or painted trim | Document the material and arrange timely assessment before damage progresses |
| Moisture tracks with rain, wind or thaw rather than cold mornings | Record weather timing and ask a qualified window/building-envelope professional to assess possible water entry |
| Fog remains visibly between panes and neither accessible surface is wet | Ask the window manufacturer or qualified glazing professional about inspection and warranty terms |
| Spreading wet material, visible growth, persistent odor or an active leak | Seek prompt qualified help; do not scrape, disturb or diagnose suspect growth |
| Water near outlets, electrical equipment, unsafe structure or suspected contamination | Keep away from the area and contact appropriate emergency or qualified services |

EPA explains that moisture control, not identification by appearance alone, is central to preventing mold problems. Its [moisture-control guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/moisture-control-guidance-building-design-construction-and-maintenance-0) discusses building-envelope and interior moisture pathways. A professional may need to distinguish overlapping causes.

## When you contact a professional

Share dated photos, the exact surface affected, any change in adjacent materials, weather patterns, and equipment/warranty details.
