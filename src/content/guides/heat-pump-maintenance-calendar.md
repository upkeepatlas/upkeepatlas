## A heat pump calendar at a glance

A heat pump can both heat and cool. The [U.S. Department of Energy's maintenance guidance](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/operating-and-maintaining-your-heat-pump) recommends filter upkeep according to manufacturer instructions and professional service at least annually. [Trane's heat pump guidance](https://www.trane.com/residential/en/resources/maintenance-tips/heat-pumps/) recommends two professional checkups, before winter heating and summer cooling. These are **different recommendations**, not a universal requirement: use the actual manual and warranty terms for your system.

| When to make a calendar entry | Safe homeowner observation or record | Professional or manufacturer-dependent work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly reminder, adjusted to your manual | Look at the accessible filter's condition, note its model and last service; check accessible vents for obstruction | Ask for help if the filter is behind a service cover or cannot be reached safely |
| After major storms or during heavy use | From safe ground level, note leaves, snow accumulation, vegetation or unusual sounds near the outdoor unit | Have a technician address persistent icing, blocked internal parts, electrical faults or damaged equipment |
| Before your local cooling season | Record cooling symptoms, indoor moisture or visible staining, and thermostat observations | Request cooling-mode assessment, airflow, coils, condensate drainage and refrigerant checks as appropriate |
| Before your local heating season | Record heating symptoms, persistent frost/ice concerns and any auxiliary-heat observations | Request heating-mode, controls and defrost checks where applicable |
| After each service visit | Save invoice, findings, model number, next recommended date and follow-up items | Have the contractor explain measured issues and which tasks the manual requires |

These entries are **planning prompts**, not automatic instructions to replace filters or clean internal equipment. Some ductless heads have washable filters; some ducted systems use replaceable media. Your manual decides how to access, clean or replace them.

## Choose your schedule by equipment, not a generic date

| If your home has… | Put this on the calendar | Verify before acting |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ducted air-source heat pump | Filter inspection reminder, cooling/heating service requests, outdoor observations | Filter type, safe access, thermostat/backup heat configuration, manufacturer service interval |
| Ductless mini-split | Separate reminders for each accessible indoor head plus outdoor-unit observations | Per-head filter cleaning method, number of heads, manual's frequency and access precautions |
| Dual-fuel heat pump plus gas furnace | Heat pump service and **separate fuel-heating safety** questions for the contractor | Whether the furnace/combustion system is included in the quoted visit |
| Ground-source (geothermal) heat pump | Indoor filter/airflow reminders and specialist service per the system documentation | Loop and fluid maintenance requirements; don't copy an outdoor air-source unit checklist |
| Unsure what system you own | Record model/serial numbers from safe paperwork or accessible labels and request the manual | Do not assume you have an outdoor unit, a filter at every head or a combustion appliance |

**Decision path:** First identify the exact model. Next, find the manual's filter and service instructions. Then note whether the equipment supplies both heating and cooling, whether it has auxiliary or fuel-fired backup, and when your area typically begins using each mode. Only then assign dates. A calendar reminder is a prompt to inspect or book service—not evidence that maintenance was performed.

## An illustrative calendar you can adapt

This example assumes a ducted, all-electric air-source heat pump serving both heating and cooling. It is not a mandated national schedule.

| Reminder | Example calendar label | Completion record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeat monthly | “Inspect accessible heat pump filter; compare with manual” | Date, condition, action taken, filter model |
| Before local cooling demand | “Request heat pump cooling-mode service” | Technician, findings, invoice and next steps |
| Before local heating demand | “Request heating/defrost and backup-heat assessment” | Technician, documented checks and follow-up |
| After storms / as conditions warrant | “Observe outdoor unit safely from ground” | Date, obstruction/ice/noise noted, whether service requested |

If your manufacturer recommends one annual professional visit, ask whether it covers both operating modes and when it should be scheduled. If it specifies two visits, make two entries. Avoid treating a contractor's generic maintenance plan as a substitute for model-specific requirements.

| Last service date and contractor | ____________________ |
| Questions about defrost, backup heat or drainage | ____________________ |
| Technician findings / measurements / follow-up | ____________________ |
| Next reminder, based on documented recommendation | ____________________ |

This worksheet is deliberately observation-first. Do not record a component as tested unless a qualified technician actually tested it.

## When the calendar should turn into a service call

Contact a qualified technician for repeated loss of heating/cooling, persistent ice that does not clear, unusual electrical smells or noises, leaks, or visible water damage. Frost during cold-weather operation may be part of normal defrost behavior, but an observation alone cannot diagnose it. Never chip ice from coils, open electrical panels, adjust refrigerant, bypass safety controls or attempt combustion work.

If the system includes a gas furnace and you suspect a gas leak, follow your utility's emergency guidance from a safe location. For a carbon monoxide alarm, follow the alarm and emergency-service instructions immediately; do not wait for a scheduled visit.
