## The three numbers on a filter label

A label such as **16 × 25 × 1** is a *nominal* size, usually in inches. The first two dimensions describe the face of the filter; the last is its nominal thickness. The physical frame can measure less than those numbers. For example, [3M Filtrete's published specifications](https://www.filtrete.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b00040201/) list nominal **16 × 25** filters with actual face dimensions around **15.69 × 24.69 inches**, and more than one actual depth for nominal 1-inch products. Those are specifications for those products, **not a conversion rule for every brand**.

| Label or observation | What it means | What to record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “16 × 25 × 1” on a filter frame | Nominal ordering size for that product | All three numbers, brand and model |
| “15.69 × 24.69 × 0.78 in” in a product specification | One manufacturer's actual physical dimensions | Exact dimensions and source |
| “16 × 25 × 4” on a different filter | A different nominal depth, not interchangeable with 1 inch | Cabinet/filter model and required depth |
| No legible label or a filter that fits poorly | Size remains **unconfirmed** | Actual measurements and equipment identifier for follow-up |

**Do not assume** every 16 × 25 × 1 filter has the same actual dimensions. [Nordic Pure's measurement guide](https://www.nordicpure.com/how-to-measure/) distinguishes labeled and measured sizes and recommends checking actual fit, especially with tight or custom slots. Manufacturer dimensions should decide between similarly labeled products.

## A safe, manual-first identification sequence

1. **Find the instructions.** Check your HVAC owner manual, filter cabinet label or the existing filter package for the approved size and type. A previous owner's filter might not be the correct one.
2. **Read the existing filter's edge.** If it is safely accessible without opening an electrical or sealed service compartment, note its three printed dimensions, manufacturer and model. Photograph the label for your own records.
3. **Compare the candidate's specifications.** Confirm nominal size **and** actual length, width and depth against the manufacturer's approved fit. If the old filter was crushed, bent or had conspicuous gaps, do not treat it as a reliable sizing template.
4. **Measure only when needed and safe.** If the label is missing, measure the old filter's *outer frame* in inches, including depth. Write the actual measurements as measured—do not silently round them into an order size. If accessible, compare against the housing opening and the equipment instructions.
5. **Resolve uncertainty before buying.** An unusual size, deep media cabinet, poor fit, inaccessible filter or disagreement between labels calls for the cabinet manufacturer or an HVAC technician to identify the correct replacement. Do not trim, stack, force or wedge filters to make them fit.

**Worked example (illustrative only):** A filter is labeled 16 × 25 × 1, but its measured frame is smaller. Record **16 × 25 × 1 as the printed nominal size** and the exact measured dimensions separately. Look up the candidate manufacturer's actual dimensions and verify the equipment instructions. Do not purchase a 15 × 24 filter simply because the old frame measured close to those numbers.

## Where the filter goes matters

Some homes have a return-grille filter, others have a filter cabinet beside the air handler, and some systems use deep media cartridges. There may be multiple return filters or a system-specific arrangement. The correct size comes from the **actual filter location and its approved instructions**, not merely from the brand name on the thermostat or outdoor unit. If you cannot identify the intended filter location safely, ask the installer or service company.

Do not operate the system with an incorrectly fitted filter as a workaround. Do not remove panels that expose wiring or moving components. Stop if the filter area is damaged, wet, contaminated or inaccessible.

## Size is separate from filtration rating and replacement timing

**Size** tells you what fits; **MERV** describes particle capture performance under a rating system. Choosing a higher rating does not fix a poor fit and may not be appropriate for every system. Consult equipment instructions for acceptable filter specifications. The [EPA's air-cleaner guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/air-cleaners-and-air-filters-home) discusses filter efficiency and system compatibility, but does not authorize overriding your equipment's requirements.
