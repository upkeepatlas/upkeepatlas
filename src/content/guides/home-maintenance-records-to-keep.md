## The five folders that answer most future questions

A useful archive is organized by **why you might need the document**, not by a pile of calendar years. Keep original signed papers where needed and searchable copies in a private backup.

| Folder | Put these records together | The question they answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home purchase and improvements | Closing statement, purchase contract, substantial project contracts, invoices, permits where applicable, payment evidence, project descriptions | What did the home cost and what changed? |
| Equipment and warranties | Model information, manuals, warranty terms, registration confirmation if applicable, purchase receipts | What equipment is installed, and what coverage may apply? |
| Maintenance and service | Service dates, contractor details, findings, work orders, paid invoices, next recommended follow-up | What was done, by whom and when? |
| Insurance and incidents | Current policy, relevant correspondence, claim numbers, dated damage observations, repair invoices | What happened and what was submitted? |
| House reference | Paint codes, filter sizes, appliance specifications, transferable manuals and non-sensitive contractor contacts | What will someone need to maintain this home? |

The [Federal Trade Commission's warranty guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties) specifically advises keeping the warranty and receipt together: the receipt can establish the purchase date and original ownership. A product registration confirmation is not a substitute for reading the actual warranty terms.

The [IRS's Publication 530](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p530) explains why purchase and improvement records matter to the home's adjusted basis. Routine repairs generally differ from improvements for tax purposes; **do not label every maintenance invoice a deductible expense or a basis increase**. Keep the original evidence and ask a tax professional when classification matters.

## Which documents should you keep longest?

This is a **decision guide**, not a statutory retention schedule. State rules, warranties, insurers, tax situations and ownership history vary.

| Record | Practical retention approach | What to check before disposal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase, closing and improvement records | Preserve throughout ownership and until any related tax or legal recordkeeping period has ended after disposition | IRS guidance, your tax professional and any ongoing dispute |
| Warranty, receipt and registration | Keep while coverage or a related claim may matter | Written coverage, claim deadlines and state consumer rules |
| Service invoice and maintenance log | Keep while the system is owned; retain longer if linked to a warranty, recurring defect or major project | Warranty service conditions, open issues and transfer needs |
| Insurance policy and claim correspondence | Keep current policy and relevant historical claim records while they may be needed | Insurer instructions, pending claims and legal obligations |
| Everyday shopping or duplicate paperwork | Review periodically rather than keeping indiscriminately | Whether it supports a return, claim, tax position or contract |

Do not delete records merely because a generic website says “three years.” The correct period can depend on when a return was filed, whether an issue remains open and the kind of record. The IRS provides separate [recordkeeping guidance for selling a home](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p523).

## A printable maintenance log example

The following is **illustrative, not an actual inspection or contractor report**. Print this table or copy the fields into a spreadsheet.

| Date | System / location | Observation or request | Work documented | Receipt / next step |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example: 2026-04-12 | HVAC / hallway return | Filter condition check due | Homeowner recorded visible condition; no equipment test claimed | Filter model noted; next reminder from manual |
| Example: 2026-05-03 | Kitchen / dishwasher | Unusual noise reported | Technician visit requested; diagnosis not yet known | Save work order and invoice when received |
| Your date: ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |
| Your date: ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ | ______ |

For every paid service, add the company name, work-order number, parts or materials listed on the invoice, any warranty on the work, and whether the issue was resolved. Record **“not checked”** instead of assuming a technician inspected something that the invoice does not document.

Use the [local-only maintenance log](/tools/maintenance-log/) to enter dates and export a CSV, or the [appliance maintenance tracker](/tools/appliance-maintenance-log/) to organize equipment-specific entries. Those tools are organizational aids, **not secure document vaults**: do not enter financial account numbers, access codes, identity documents or other sensitive details.

## Warranty packet checklist

For each major appliance, HVAC component or installed product, collect:

- [ ] Manufacturer and model; serial number stored securely, not on a public/shared checklist
- [ ] Dated purchase receipt or invoice and proof of purchase
- [ ] Exact warranty terms and any applicable installation or labor coverage
- [ ] Installation date and installer documentation, if relevant
- [ ] Registration confirmation, if completed, and any claim correspondence
- [ ] Maintenance/service invoices required to support a specific claim, where applicable
- [ ] Contact details and the claim procedure stated in the warranty

A warranty may have exclusions or maintenance conditions. **Do not assume registration is always required** or that a service contract is the same as a manufacturer warranty. Read the product-specific document; the [FTC explains the distinction](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties).

## A simple naming and backup method

Name each file `YYYY-MM-DD_system_document-type_vendor.pdf` (for example, `2026-05-03_dishwasher_service-invoice.pdf`). Keep one index row pointing to the file, rather than pasting sensitive receipts into a public calendar. Scan fading thermal receipts promptly, check that scans are legible, and keep a separate protected backup. Limit sharing to what a contractor, insurer or buyer actually needs.

For paper documents, a labeled folder or binder works. For digital copies, use a trusted storage location with account protection and a backup you can recover. If a file contains signatures, addresses, account details or serial numbers, restrict access. Upkeep Atlas does not collect or store your uploaded documents through this guide.

## When to request professional help

A record can help a qualified technician understand symptoms; it cannot diagnose gas, electrical, structural, roof or moisture hazards. Do not open panels, climb roofs or investigate suspected gas leaks to complete a log. For urgent hazards follow local emergency and utility guidance first. Ask a tax professional or attorney about legal retention, insurance disputes and basis treatment rather than relying on this general article.
