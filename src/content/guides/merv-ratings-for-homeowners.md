## Read the rating, then check the system

MERV is a **particle-filtration performance rating**, not a measure of fresh-air ventilation, filter lifetime, indoor-air quality in your particular room, or equipment compatibility. The rating comes from a standardized test method developed by ASHRAE for particles approximately **0.3 to 10 micrometers** across. See the [EPA's definition of MERV](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/what-merv-rating).

The [EPA's Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) recommends selecting **MERV 13 or as high as the system fan and filter slot can accommodate**. That qualification matters. A filter that is physically the correct size can still have an unsuitable airflow resistance for a specific installation. Check the equipment manual, filter-cabinet requirements and manufacturer specifications; ask an HVAC professional if compatibility is unclear.

## A comparison that separates capture from compatibility

The following figures are **minimum laboratory capture criteria for the stated particle-size bands**, not predictions of how much dust or smoke will disappear from your house. A dash means no specified minimum for that band; it does *not* mean the filter captures nothing.

| Rating | 0.3–1 µm particles | 1–3 µm particles | 3–10 µm particles | Homeowner interpretation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| MERV 8 | — | ≥20% | ≥70% | Captures many larger particles; no minimum specified here for the smallest band |
| MERV 11 | ≥20% | ≥65% | ≥85% | More effective across smaller particle bands than MERV 8 |
| MERV 13 | ≥50% | ≥85% | ≥90% | EPA's preferred upgrade target **when the system can accommodate it** |

The figures describe the MERV rating criteria summarized in [EPA's filter guidance](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) and ASHRAE's rating method. They do not compare pressure drop between two actual products: filters with the same MERV can differ in dimensions, media area and airflow resistance. Always compare the **specific product's** published specifications and your equipment requirements.

### Three decisions before buying

| Your situation | Sensible next step | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You know the approved size and filter specification | Find a correctly sized filter within the equipment's permitted ratings; consider MERV 13 if compatible | Assuming a higher number automatically improves the whole home's air |
| You want better fine-particle filtration, but the manual is silent | Ask the equipment manufacturer or an HVAC technician about the system's filter slot and airflow capacity | Guessing that every 1-inch MERV 13 product will work identically |
| You have a deep media cabinet or unusual cartridge | Match the cabinet's specified model, actual dimensions and rating | Substituting a thin filter, stacking filters or forcing a cartridge into place |
| Your new filter bends, whistles, leaves gaps, or airflow seems abnormal | Stop treating the upgrade as confirmed; seek qualified assessment | Continuing with a poorly fitted or potentially restrictive filter |

**Important distinction:** A correctly installed MERV 13 filter is not a HEPA filter. Also, HVAC filtration only treats air that passes through the filter while the system fan operates. A filter alone cannot solve every indoor-air contaminant or ventilation problem. The EPA discusses portable air cleaners as an alternative when a forced-air system cannot accommodate higher-efficiency filtration.

## MERV, MPR and FPR are different scales

MERV is the standardized rating described above. **MPR** and **FPR** are proprietary filter-label systems; their numbers are not MERV values and should not be compared numerically. The [EPA's air-cleaner guide](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) identifies these different rating systems. Look for a product's actual MERV rating and the manufacturer's specifications instead of relying on an approximate conversion chart.

## Keep rating, size and inspection separate

The *rating* describes particle capture, the *dimensions* determine physical fit, and the *maintenance interval* depends on the filter and equipment instructions plus real conditions. Use the [filter-size identification guide](/guides/how-to-find-hvac-filter-size/) before ordering, the [HVAC filter inspection guide](/guides/how-often-check-hvac-filter/) to distinguish checking from replacement, and the [HVAC filter schedule tool](/tools/hvac-filter-schedule/) to keep reminders. Browse the [homeowner guide library](/guides/) for related care decisions.

Do not remove electrical or sealed service panels to investigate a filter. If the slot is inaccessible, damaged, wet, or shows signs of abnormal airflow, have a qualified HVAC professional assess it. Filter selection is not a substitute for addressing a known indoor-air pollution source.
