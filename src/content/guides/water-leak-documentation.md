## Safety first: when not to document up close

**Do not enter standing water near electrical equipment**, touch wet outlets or appliances, or reach for a breaker or valve through water. If there may be sewage, chemical contamination, a damaged ceiling, or unsafe structure, stay clear and contact appropriate emergency or qualified services. If water is actively flowing or spreading, contact an emergency plumber or property manager promptly. Only use a shutoff you already know how to operate **safely**, following its instructions; do not dismantle plumbing to locate a leak.

The [EPA's moisture guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) emphasizes promptly correcting the water source and drying wet materials. Its general drying timeframes are not a safe waiting period for an active leak. For a flooded property, the [EPA's flooded-home guidance](https://www.epa.gov/emergencies-iaq/text-version-flooded-home) warns of electrical, structural and contamination hazards.

## The six-part handoff: what a plumber actually needs to know

This original **observation-to-question matrix** separates what you can reliably report from what a professional must determine. Record only what you can see without opening walls, moving appliances or intentionally reproducing a leak.

| Record this | Useful detail | Avoid assuming | Ask the plumber |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Where** | Room, fixture, cabinet/floor/wall position; wide photo showing context | The nearest pipe must be the source | What locations need assessment? |
| **When** | First noticed date/time; last known dry observation if known | First discovery equals the start of the leak | What evidence can narrow the timeline? |
| **What you see** | Drip, pooling, stain, swelling, odor; whether active now | A stain identifies a broken fitting | What is the likely source after inspection? |
| **Ordinary-use pattern** | Whether it appeared after normal sink, shower or appliance use | A correlation proves the failed component | Which systems should be checked? |
| **Changes** | New versus old photo, spreading area, affected adjacent room | No visible change means no concealed moisture | Are hidden materials affected? |
| **Actions and contacts** | Safe shutoff used, plumber/property manager called, reference numbers | A temporary dry patch means resolved | What follow-up, drying or specialist help is needed? |

**Photo sequence:** Take one wide shot from a safe doorway or dry floor, one closer shot of the visible wet area, and a later comparison from the same position if safe. Note the time of each photo and describe the room and surface. Do not climb, enter a flooded room or remove covers to obtain a better angle. Keep originals if you may need them for a property or insurance record, and avoid sharing identifying details unnecessarily.

## Choose the next call by the situation

| Situation | Practical next contact |
| --- | --- |
| Active flow, spreading water or worsening material damage | Prompt plumber or building emergency contact; prioritize safe source control |
| Wet area near electricity, suspected sewage or chemical contamination, unstable ceiling | Stay out; appropriate emergency service and qualified specialists |
| Recurring dampness, odor or stain without obvious active flow | Timely plumbing/building-envelope assessment; do not assume the origin |
| Shared wall, unit above/below or managed building | Property manager/HOA or landlord as applicable, alongside qualified help |
| Water damage affecting belongings or building materials | Ask the relevant insurer about its **own** documentation and notification requirements; coverage is not guaranteed |

A plumber may address the water source while a separate restoration or building professional assesses wet materials. The [EPA's mold and moisture guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) explains why fixing the source matters even when surface water is no longer visible.

## Two hypothetical handoffs

**Example A — slow cabinet dampness:** “Tuesday at 7 p.m., the back-right base of the kitchen sink cabinet looked damp. I have a wide photo and a closer one. It was dry-looking on Monday, but I did not check beneath the cabinet. No water is currently pooling. I have not opened or tightened any fittings.” This tells a plumber what was observed without naming a failed pipe.

**Example B — active ceiling drip:** “At 6:20 a.m., water began dripping from a downstairs ceiling below a bathroom. The ceiling looks swollen. We have stayed out of the affected area and contacted the building emergency number.” Do **not** stand beneath a weakened ceiling to photograph it. These examples are fictional, not actual inspections.

### Questions to ask after assessment

- What source was confirmed, and what remains uncertain?
- Are surrounding or concealed materials wet, and who should evaluate them?
- What repair was performed, what should be monitored, and what documentation can be provided?
- Is any further specialist assessment or property-manager coordination necessary?
