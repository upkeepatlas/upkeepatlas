## Three different pools of money—not one vague emergency number

A home repair reserve is **not** the same as your general household emergency fund, and neither is the same as planned replacement savings. You may hold them in the same financial institution, but record their intended uses separately so you do not count the same dollars twice.

| Pool | Intended use | Example trigger | Planning basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Home repair reserve | An unexpected, necessary home repair | A sudden plumbing failure needing qualified service | Known vulnerabilities, local quotes, cash on hand and deductibles |
| Planned replacement fund | Work you can anticipate and schedule | An aging water heater assessed for replacement | Current project estimate, existing savings and a flexible horizon |
| Household emergency fund | Broader disruption to essential expenses | Job loss or unexpected household expense | Essential living costs and your household's circumstances |

The [Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's emergency-fund guide](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/saving-money/answers/key-terms/emergency-fund/) explains that emergency savings are for unplanned expenses and financial emergencies. It does **not** specify a universal dollar amount for every home's repair reserve. Keep your own categories clear.

## Set a target from exposure, not a guessed national average

Work through four questions. They turn a vague savings rule into a decision you can revisit.

1. **What could fail without warning?** Inventory the essential systems you actually have: heating or cooling, water supply and drainage, roof, and major appliances. Note age and known concerns, but do not diagnose a hazardous defect yourself.
2. **What do you know about the potential bill?** Save recent invoices, written contractor estimates, equipment documentation and any applicable service agreements. A neighbor's bill or a generic online average is not a quote for your home.
3. **What might insurance or a warranty actually cover?** Read your own policy exclusions and deductibles and the written warranty terms. Do not assume a mechanical failure, gradual leak or maintenance issue is covered.
4. **How much can you set aside without neglecting other essentials?** Choose an initial cash milestone, then review it after a professional assessment or real repair bill. An incomplete reserve is a planning gap, not a reason to delay urgent safety work.

A fixed 1% or 3% of property value cannot tell you whether a $250,000 home has a recently replaced system or a serious existing defect. Home price, repair scope, insurance terms and local labor costs are different variables. The [Department of Energy's residential efficiency measures database](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/buildings/national-residential-efficiency-measures-database) describes variability in building-measure costs; it does not endorse a universal emergency-fund percentage.

## Worked example: an illustrative reserve gap

Imagine a homeowner chooses a **$4,800 provisional repair-reserve target** after reviewing their own circumstances. They have **$1,200** already reserved and can contribute **$150 per month**. These amounts are hypothetical, not measured U.S. repair costs or a recommended target.

| Calculation | Illustrative result |
| --- | ---: |
| Provisional reserve target | $4,800 |
| Current reserve balance | $1,200 |
| Remaining gap: $4,800 − $1,200 | $3,600 |
| Monthly contribution | $150 |
| Months to reach target: $3,600 ÷ $150 | **24 months** |

This assumes no withdrawals, interest, taxes or changes to the target. If a necessary $900 repair occurs after six deposits, the balance becomes $1,200 + (6 × $150) − $900 = **$1,200**. The gap returns to $3,600, or another 24 months at $150 per month. A repair does not mean the plan failed; it means the reserve was used for its purpose.

**Reusable formula:** months to goal = max(0, target − current balance) ÷ monthly contribution, rounded *up* to the next whole month. If monthly contribution is zero and a gap remains, there is no finite completion date. Recalculate after each withdrawal or new quote.

## What to do when the reserve is too small

| Situation | Sensible next decision | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Known non-urgent work | Obtain written scope and estimates; adjust the timing and savings plan | Do not label a delay safe without a qualified assessment |
| Sudden essential-system failure | Contact an appropriate qualified provider and check policy/warranty terms | Do not attempt hazardous gas, electrical, roof or structural work |
| Repair quote exceeds available cash | Ask for an itemized scope and explore legitimate payment options and assistance | Financing has terms and costs; compare total repayment before agreeing |
| Money was withdrawn | Record the amount and reason, then restart the contribution calculation | Do not double-count money already assigned to another emergency |
| The household has no room to save yet | Choose a realistic first milestone and track the gap | A universal percentage is not a substitute for household budgeting |

The [FTC's warranty guidance](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/warranties) is useful when checking written coverage, limitations and receipts. It does not establish whether an insurer will pay a specific claim. For financial hardship, the [CFPB's savings resources](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/saving-money/) can help you plan a starting point.

## A one-page reserve review worksheet

Copy or print this table and review it after a repair, a policy change, a major purchase or a new professional assessment. Avoid storing bank credentials or account numbers in shared worksheets.

| Question | Your answer |
| --- | --- |
| Essential systems with known concerns | __________ |
| Recent written quotes or assessment dates | __________ |
| Relevant deductible, warranty limits or exclusions to verify | __________ |
| Provisional repair-reserve target and why | $__________ / __________ |
| Current balance reserved for repairs only | $__________ |
| Sustainable monthly contribution | $__________ |
| Gap and estimated months using the formula | $__________ / __________ |
| Date to review again | __________ |

**What this worksheet cannot do:** predict a roof failure, replace a professional inspection, establish insurance coverage or recommend a financial product. Home type, climate, equipment, family finances and local rules change the decision. For active leaks, suspected gas hazards, electrical faults or structural concerns, seek appropriate emergency or qualified professional help promptly rather than waiting for the savings target.
