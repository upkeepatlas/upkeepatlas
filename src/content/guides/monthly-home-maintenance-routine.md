## The ten-minute reset (an illustrative time budget)

The **ten minutes is a planning target, not a measured completion time**. Stop early if you discover a concern; documenting it matters more than finishing every row. Use a phone note or the [local-only maintenance log](/tools/maintenance-log/).

| Approximate time | Look or decide | Write down |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 minutes | Look under accessible sinks and around visible plumbing and appliances for new drips, staining or dampness. Do not move heavy appliances or open panels. | Location and what changed; photograph only if safe. |
| 2 minutes | Walk through occupied rooms and note new ceiling stains, musty odors, persistent condensation or blocked accessible vents. | What you saw, when, and whether it is getting worse. |
| 2 minutes | Check your HVAC filter *reminder* and the equipment manual. Inspect the filter only when its access procedure is safe and familiar. | Filter size, last check and the next manual-based action. |
| 2 minutes | Review smoke/CO alarm instructions and their test dates; perform any due user test as the manufacturer directs. | Device, test date and any failure needing prompt attention. |
| 2 minutes | Choose the most important unresolved item and assign a next step: monitor, schedule a qualified professional, or follow a relevant manual. | One action, owner and reminder date. |

These are suggested observation slots, **not mandatory monthly service intervals**. For example, filter checks and alarm testing should follow the relevant product guidance; don't replace filters or batteries simply because a generic checklist says to.

## Make the routine fit your home

Use this decision table to remove irrelevant tasks rather than accumulating them.

| Your situation | Keep in your short monthly review | Put elsewhere |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apartment or condo | Visible interior leaks, accessible alarms and your own equipment reminders | Shared roof, gutters and central systems managed by the association or landlord |
| Home with a basement or recurring dampness | Accessible signs of water or odor, with notes on change over time | Moisture diagnosis, concealed spaces and remediation for a qualified professional |
| Ductless heat pump or unusual HVAC | Model-specific filter and service reminders | Generic furnace filter replacement advice |
| Home with no gas-burning equipment | Alarm requirements based on your actual devices and local rules | Assumptions that every home has the same CO hazards or equipment |
| Limited mobility or very little time | One accessible room and a review of open issues | Any climbing, lifting or reaching beyond a safe position |

If you have no relevant item in a category, mark it **not applicable**, not “done.” The routine is intentionally small; seasonal projects belong in the [seasonal maintenance builder](/tools/seasonal-maintenance-builder/), and model-specific reminders belong in the [appliance maintenance tracker](/tools/appliance-maintenance-log/).

## A reusable three-line record

Copy this small template each month. The entries below are examples, **not observations from a real house**.

| Date and area | Status | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Example: May 4 — kitchen sink cabinet | No new visible moisture; accessible area only | Recheck next routine |
| Example: May 4 — hall ceiling | New discoloration noticed; cause unknown | Record photo and arrange qualified evaluation |
| Your date and area: ______ | Not checked / unchanged / changed: ______ | Person and date: ______ |

A status of “not checked” is useful information. Never mark an inaccessible crawlspace, roof, electrical panel or combustion system as inspected because you walked past it.

## What does not belong in a ten-minute check

A monthly reminder is **not** permission to climb onto roofs or ladders, handle electrical wiring, disassemble gas appliances, disturb suspect asbestos/lead materials, or diagnose serious moisture damage. For a suspected gas leak or other immediate danger, leave the area and follow your utility's and local emergency authority's instructions. For a failed alarm, follow its manufacturer instructions promptly; do not defer life-safety issues until next month.

The [EPA's moisture and mold guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) emphasizes controlling moisture. Observing a stain can prompt action, but it cannot establish the hidden cause or prove a space is safe. The [Department of Energy's home energy checklist](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/home-energy-checklist) includes recurring energy-awareness habits and professional heating/cooling maintenance; it is not a universal service schedule for every house.

## Keep one reminder, not twelve overlapping lists

Pick a repeatable calendar date. Keep a single running “open issues” note; if an issue is unresolved, carry it forward with a concrete next step rather than adding it as a fresh task each month. Review seasonal needs separately, particularly before extreme weather. If a manufacturer, local authority, insurer or qualified technician specifies a different schedule, that specific requirement takes precedence over this optional routine.
