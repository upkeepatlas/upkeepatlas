## A cabinet-zone checklist you can use without tools

This original observation map moves from **what is visible** to **what to record**. It is not a diagnostic test, and the closest pipe is not necessarily the source. Look only from a stable, dry floor; do not move plumbing, appliances, electrical cords or suspect contaminated materials.

| Zone visible from the cabinet opening | Signs to note | Useful record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Upper cabinet / underside of sink** | Droplets, staining or dampness around the basin and countertop underside | Photo from the doorway, exact spot, whether the countertop or sink rim is wet |
| **Exposed supply lines and shutoff area** | Water beads, drip marks, mineral residue or corrosion | Which visible line or area, active dripping versus old marks; do not tighten fittings to test |
| **Visible drain and disposal area, if present** | Drips, residue or dampness on the cabinet floor beneath | Whether moisture was noticed after ordinary sink or dishwasher use, without deliberately provoking a leak |
| **Back wall and pipe openings** | Darkening, peeling finish, musty odor or damp-looking edges | Whether the mark grows or returns; photograph the same angle later |
| **Cabinet base, corners and toe-kick edge** | Puddles, warped laminate, raised seams, swelling or soft-looking board | Approximate area and date; avoid pressing weakened material or pulling off panels |
| **Adjacent floor or room below** | New flooring discoloration, lifted edges or ceiling stain | Location relative to sink and change over time; seek evaluation rather than assuming the leak path |

A musty smell or discoloration is a **reason to investigate moisture**, not proof of mold species or water origin. The [U.S. EPA explains that controlling moisture is central to mold prevention](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## Three observation moments

### 1. When you first notice it

Record the date and time. From a safe position, take one wide photo showing the whole cabinet and one closer photo of the affected area. Write down whether you see standing water, active drips, damp stored items, stains or changes in the cabinet finish. If cleaning chemicals are leaking or mixed with water, do not handle the mixture.

If water is near an outlet, powered disposal, dishwasher wiring or another electrical hazard, **keep clear** and contact qualified help. If water is flowing or spreading, promptly contact a plumber or property manager and follow only the fixture's known, safe manufacturer shutoff instructions; do not reach through water to operate electrical equipment.

### 2. At the next safe observation

If the area is safe to revisit, compare the same viewpoint. Is the mark larger? Does a dry-looking patch become damp again? Was the sink or an adjacent appliance used normally since the last photo? A timing pattern can help a plumber narrow the investigation, but it does **not** prove which connection or surface failed.

Avoid running the faucet or dishwasher repeatedly to reproduce a suspected leak. Do not open walls, remove the disposal, loosen plumbing, or place a fan near potentially contaminated water or unsafe electricity.

### 3. When arranging assessment

Send the dated photos, exact location, whether water is active, visible changes to cabinet materials, and any known timing pattern. Ask the professional to identify the source **and assess affected materials**, including concealed areas if warranted. For shared buildings, follow the owner/HOA/property-manager notification process rather than assuming responsibility.

The [EPA moisture guide](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) recommends prompt action on leaks and wet materials. Its general 24–48-hour drying guidance is **not** permission to wait that long before responding, nor a guarantee that mold will not develop.

## Which observation changes the urgency?

| Situation | Sensible next action |
| --- | --- |
| One dry, unchanged old mark with no current moisture observed | Photograph and document; ask about past repairs if records exist; do not certify it as harmless |
| New dampness, recurring droplets or a persistent odor | Arrange timely plumbing/moisture assessment and record changes |
| Active drip, pooling, swelling or spreading stain | Seek prompt qualified help to stop the source and evaluate damage |
| Water near electricity, suspected sewage/chemicals, or unsafe/unstable cabinet materials | Keep away, prevent exposure and contact appropriate emergency or qualified services |

**Hypothetical comparison:** In home A, a cabinet has a faint old ring that remains dry across two ordinary observations; its cause remains unknown. In home B, water pools beneath the cabinet after normal use and the base is visibly swelling; the owner contacts a plumber promptly and provides photos. These are examples, not field inspections or guarantees.
