## The two-season difference at a glance

[ENERGY STAR recommends professional pre-season checks](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist): cooling equipment ahead of summer, heating equipment ahead of winter. This is a planning guide, not a service procedure. A heat pump that heats and cools may need attention to both operating modes; follow its manufacturer's schedule.

| Question | Spring: before sustained cooling | Fall: before sustained heating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What are you preparing for? | Cooling demand and moisture removal | Heating demand and, where applicable, combustion safety |
| What can you observe without opening equipment? | Thermostat cooling setting, visible water staining, unobstructed accessible vents, outdoor-unit surroundings from ground level | Thermostat heating setting, unusual sounds or odors, accessible vents, visible equipment condition |
| What should the professional assess? | Cooling coils, condensate drainage, refrigerant performance and airflow as appropriate | Heating operation and controls; for fuel-fired equipment, combustion, gas/oil connections and heat exchanger |
| What belongs in both visits? | Filter condition, thermostat operation, electrical/control and airflow assessment by the qualified technician | The same shared checks, plus heating-specific inspection |
| What should you record? | Date, cooling symptoms, filter model, contractor findings and follow-up | Date, heating symptoms, alarm concerns, contractor findings and follow-up |

The professional categories above come from [ENERGY STAR's maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist). Not every home has a furnace, refrigerant-based cooling system, ducts or an outdoor unit. Ask for service matched to your equipment rather than buying a generic checklist.

## Your safe, observation-only preparation

Before either visit, identify your system type and model from an accessible label or existing paperwork. Find its manual and warranty/service recommendations. Note the filter's current size, model and inspection date; inspect or replace it **only when the manufacturer provides a safe, accessible method**. ENERGY STAR recommends checking filters monthly, but actual replacement depends on the product and conditions. Use our [HVAC filter inspection guide](/guides/how-often-check-hvac-filter/) and [filter reminder tool](/tools/hvac-filter-schedule/) instead of guessing a universal replacement interval.

In spring, note whether the cooling setting works as expected under appropriate outdoor conditions and whether you see water stains around accessible equipment. From the ground, note plants or loose debris close to an outdoor unit; do not open cabinets, reach into fans or clean electrical components. In fall, note whether the heating setting responds normally and whether you notice persistent abnormal smells, noise or uneven comfort. Do not test by overriding safeties or removing service covers.

If you smell gas, follow your gas utility's emergency instructions and contact emergency services from a safe location; do not operate switches or investigate the equipment. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, follow the alarm and local emergency guidance immediately. Do not wait for a seasonal appointment. Any suspected electrical fault, refrigerant leak, combustion issue, water damage or inaccessible filter is professional territory.

## Choose the right service request for your system

| Your equipment | What to ask when booking | What not to assume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Central AC plus gas/oil furnace | Cooling assessment before hot weather; heating and combustion-safety assessment before cold weather | A spring AC visit automatically included furnace combustion checks |
| Air-source heat pump | Manufacturer-specified service covering cooling **and** heating operation, including defrost-related concerns when applicable | A furnace-style burner inspection applies to an all-electric heat pump |
| Electric resistance furnace or air handler | Heating controls and airflow checks appropriate to the model | Fuel-burner or gas-pressure tests apply |
| Ductless mini-split | Manufacturer-specific indoor/outdoor maintenance and both operating modes where provided | A central-duct filter schedule applies unchanged |
| Boiler or other hydronic heating | Equipment-specific professional heating service | A forced-air filter or AC coil checklist describes this system |

**Worked planning example (illustrative, not a prescribed interval):** A homeowner with a separate central AC and gas furnace makes two calendar entries: “before local cooling season—AC service” and “before local heating season—furnace service.” They attach the manual's requirements and last invoice to each entry. A neighbor with one reversible heat pump instead asks the contractor which seasonal checks its model requires, rather than automatically purchasing two identical tune-ups.

## Questions to ask before approving work

Ask whether the proposed visit covers the relevant operating mode, which checks are included, whether the technician will document any concerning findings and what the manufacturer recommends for your exact model. A checklist is not proof that a component passed inspection: ask for the actual finding and any recommended next step. Do not assume every suggested replacement is necessary without a clear explanation.
