## Check condition instead of blindly following a date

[ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling) recommends checking filters monthly, particularly during busy heating and cooling seasons, and replacing them when dirty. Its general guidance mentions a three-month maximum, but your filter type, equipment manual, household conditions and usage may call for a different interval.

Record the filter size, type, location and date of the last inspection or change. Keeping a photo of the package or model number can prevent accidentally purchasing the wrong replacement.

## Make the process safe and repeatable

Read your system's manual for its approved procedure and safe access points. Never open an electrical compartment or disassemble a heating system just to reach a filter. If access is difficult, ask a qualified service contractor for instructions.

Keep removed filters and dust away from living areas. If you discover significant moisture or suspected contamination, address the source and seek appropriate professional advice rather than improvising repairs. See the [ENERGY STAR maintenance checklist](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) and the [EPA moisture guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

## Professional servicing is a separate reminder

[ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/saveathome/heating-cooling/maintenance-checklist) recommends qualified seasonal HVAC maintenance and suggests scheduling cooling inspection before summer and heating inspection before winter. These checks are not the same as changing a filter and may involve safety-critical work.

Keep maintenance invoices and any follow-up recommendations in the same home log as filter checks. Your contractor can explain which manufacturer-specific tasks belong on your calendar.

## Adapt the reminder based on experience

If a filter becomes dirty earlier than expected, consult the manual or a contractor about changing your inspection schedule. If it remains clean, continue to honor manufacturer requirements. A generic calendar should not outweigh the instructions for your actual system.
